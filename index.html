About LYF Matters Globally

The common denominator.

There is a wound that lives in every city, every boardroom, every classroom, and every mirror. It begins before we have words for it. A child learns their skin is wrong, their family structure is broken, their emotions are too much.

By twelve, self-esteem drops. Seven out of ten girls and boys believe they are not enough. Not attractive enough, not smart enough, not worthy of taking up space. It whispers the same lie in a thousand languages.

That is the common denominator. Not our differences, but our shared breaking. Not our cultures, but our collective forgetting of who we were before the world taught us to doubt our worth.

The wound begins in childhood. So the repair must too.

What looks like a leadership crisis in adults is a self-concept crisis that started in elementary school. Professional plateaus are emotional ceilings built on playgrounds. The anxiety that stalls an executive first showed up at seven, when they learned their feelings were inconvenient.

So the work spans a whole life. Picture books that tell children they matter. The Crayon Theory, which helps adults unlearn what they absorbed before they could spell it. We are not only treating symptoms in boardrooms. We are preventing them in classrooms.

When a foster child sees a family that looks like theirs honored on the page, when an immigrant child learns their multilingual mind is a strength, when an anxious child discovers their feelings have names, one life changes and a cycle breaks.

How it works

Prevention

Children get emotional tools and cultural pride now, so limiting beliefs never calcify. A child who learns to regulate at five is not an adult learning it at thirty-five.

Intervention

For adults who never got those tools, The Crayon Theory excavates the childhood programming and rewrites the internal narrative. It is not too late.

Interruption

Healed adults become parents, teachers, and leaders who do not pass the wound forward. That is how a generational cycle actually breaks.

The Founder

Keyonna Monroe

Executive Producer. Webby Award winner, Emmy nominee. Founder and Executive Director of Pretty2Me.org.

KM

A proud alumna of Spelman College and USC, with degrees in Broadcast Journalism and Communications. She began in commercial advertising with Ford, worked as a sports journalist at ESPN, and became an Executive Producer at NBC Universal. She is certified in Neuro-Linguistic Programming and Emotional Intelligence.

She is also a survivor of child trafficking and domestic violence. She rose out of poverty in South Central Los Angeles and became an advocate for underserved communities and trauma survivors worldwide. Her LYF initiative, short for Loving Yourself First, grew out of that.

Through the LYF Matters Project she works on unlearning conditioned unworthiness, rewriting harmful narratives, and healing generational trauma. Her advocacy has helped create safe houses in Ghana, Rwanda, and Uganda, and shelters in Atlanta and Manhattan.

She is an acclaimed children's book author who uses storytelling to give young people emotional awareness, character, and resilience. Her voice is not just heard. It is felt.

N2ME Framework

Four levels of intimacy with yourself.

01Awareness

Name the pattern without flinching. You cannot change a thing you refuse to look at directly.

02Acceptance

Stop arguing with what is already true. Acceptance is not approval. It is the ground you stand on to move.

03Action

Small, repeatable, unglamorous. The work that changes a life rarely looks impressive from outside.

04Alignment

When the inside and outside finally match, and you stop performing a version of yourself for the room.
